## The brief

> Pick one piece of work you actually made. Find every file that is a version
> of it. Document how they are related.

A file family is everything that descends from one piece of work: the
original, the copies, the exports to other formats, the screenshot someone
took of it, the version emailed to somebody and edited there, the backup, the
one renamed when you could not tell which was current. Six members is a
small family. Most real ones are larger.

The work is in the classification, not the collection. Copying is not
exporting; an export is a conversion with a new format and usually a loss.
A snapshot is a copy taken deliberately at a moment; a dead end is a branch
nobody continued. Naming which is which forces the distinction to be made on
evidence — file dates, formats, content — rather than on memory, which is
the part this audit is testing.

Expect at least one relationship you cannot establish. Two files exist,
they are clearly related, and nothing available to you says which came from
which. Mark it as unestablished and say what evidence would have settled it.
That is a finding. Filling the gap with a plausible guess is not.

## What you submit

One document containing the genealogy — a diagram or an annotated list, either
is fine — and the classification and evidence for each member. Use the real
filenames. If a filename would disclose something you would rather not submit,
say so and use a consistent substitute throughout; do not silently tidy the
names, because the names are evidence.
