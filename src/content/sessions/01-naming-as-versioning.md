## Before the session

Go looking, not guessing. Check a downloads folder, a shared drive, an email
thread with attachments, a folder of assignment drafts: anywhere files
accumulate without anyone enforcing a naming policy. Bring three real
filenames that carry some version-like signal in the name itself: a trailing
number, "final," "old," "backup," a date, a set of initials, a
`(1)` a browser added on your behalf.

Do not invent examples. The point is that this is common enough that you
won't have to.

## In the session

Each signal gets read out and sorted: what problem was it solving? Some are
answering "which is current," some "who touched this," some "am I allowed to
delete the other one." A few will turn out to be answering nothing at all:
noise left over from a system that auto-renamed something and nobody
noticed.

## Afterwards

Keep the three filenames. Later weeks ask what a version-control system
would have recorded automatically, for the same three files, and how much of
your naming scheme survives once it does.
