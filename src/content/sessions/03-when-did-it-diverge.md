## Before the session

Find a file on your own machine with a name that admits it's a copy — "Copy
of," "(1)," a forwarded attachment saved under the sender's filename,
anything duplicated rather than newly created. Locate the file it was copied
from, or your best reconstruction of what that file must have looked like at
the moment of copying.

Open both. Compare them properly, not by eye alone — line by line in a text
file, cell by cell in a spreadsheet, slide by slide in a deck.

## In the session

Some pairs turn out identical: a copy nobody ever touched, still just
clutter. Bring one of those too if you have one — the negative case matters
as much as the positive one. For pairs that diverged, the group works
backward from the differences to the single change most likely to be the
first one, and argues about whether that change was the moment the copy
became something else, or just the first of several before that moment
arrived.

## Afterwards

Whichever pair you brought, keep the specific change you identified. Next
week is about what happens after that point — once a copy has diverged and
someone has to decide what to do with two related, unreconciled versions.
