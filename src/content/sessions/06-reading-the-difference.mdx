This lab is two exercises, both done here on the page. There is nothing to
bring. Work through them in order: the second one depends on having seen
what the first one refuses to tell you.

## One: read a diff of this site

The syllabus you are enrolled under has been revised once. Its version header
says so, and says what changed. Below is the machine's account of the same
revision, computed from the two week lists by the method in the lecture.

<DiffViewer />

Write down two things. First, the thing the diff reports accurately, which is
worth stating plainly because it is genuinely useful. Second, the thing it
cannot report: read the version header on the [syllabus](/syllabus/) and
compare what a person wrote about that revision with what the comparison
produced. The gap between the two is the subject of this week.

One of the five differences it reports is not a difference. Find it before
you read the note underneath, and say what would have to change about the
comparison for it to know.

## Two: decide which file is current

The diff told you where two versions differ. It did not tell you which to
keep, because that is not a question about differences. Here is a folder
where you have to answer it anyway.

<WhichFileIsCurrent />

Choose before you reveal the reasoning. Then write down the ground you were
actually using (recency, lineage, corroboration, or the filename) and
whether the reasoning changed your mind. If it did not, say what would have.

## Afterwards

Identify the one artefact in that package that nobody created on purpose,
and what it establishes that no deliberately written file in the folder does.
That artefact is the whole lesson: the most reliable evidence in a folder is
usually the residue of an operation rather than anything a person chose to
record.

This package is a small, resolvable version of the
[Which One Is Real?](/assessments/which-one-is-real/) assessment. That one is
larger, some of its evidence contradicts itself, and it does not show you the
answer.
