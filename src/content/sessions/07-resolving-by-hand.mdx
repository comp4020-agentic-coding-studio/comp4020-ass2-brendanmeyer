## The report

Two people edited the same committee report independently. Most of their
edits combined without incident, which is the ordinary case and not
interesting. Three did not.

## What the exercise is doing

It will not record a resolution until you have written a reason. That is not
a formality bolted on to make the exercise longer. A merged document has no
field for why, so a resolution that is never written down survives only in
the memory of whoever made it, and the file that results is indistinguishable
from text nobody ever argued about. Requiring the reason is the only way the
reasoning enters the record at all.

One of the three cannot be resolved from the text you are given. Keeping both
versions produces a document that contradicts itself, and choosing between
them means asserting something about a meeting you did not attend. The
resolution there is to mark it unresolved and name the evidence that would
settle it. An unresolved conflict, stated as unresolved, is a finding. A
conflict closed by picking whichever version read better is a fabrication.

## Afterwards

Classify each of the three as mechanical, substantive, or defensible either
way, and check your classification against the note the exercise reveals
after you record. Then read your own three reasons together. The transcript
the exercise produces is exactly the form the
[Merge Conflict, In Writing](/assessments/merge-conflict-in-writing/)
assessment asks for — the merged text, and the reasoning that produced it,
kept as two separate things.
