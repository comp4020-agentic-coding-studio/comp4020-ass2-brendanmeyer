# Copies & Clones

Two files pass every identity test. Then someone edits one.

---

## Duplication

Read the bytes. Write them somewhere else. Done.

A copy nobody touches is not a derivative of anything. It's clutter.

---

## Derivation

The claim that a copy now has its own purpose, its own audience, its own
errors.

Nothing in the filesystem marks the transition.

---

## Five people, five thresholds

- one edit
- a meaningful edit
- independent circulation
- a different audience
- enough divergence that reconciling would take real effort

None of these is a property of the file. Two can be satisfied while the
bytes stay identical.

---

## Why there's no test for it

Derivation is a claim about how a file will be used from now on.

The bytes record what has happened. No property of what has happened can
encode what is about to.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> The judgement has consequences a hash can't settle: who owns the result,
> which version a reader should trust.

---

## Librarians split the difference

Cataloguing gives one creation four separate levels:

- **work** — the creation itself
- **expression** — one realisation of it
- **manifestation** — one published form of that
- **item** — the single copy on the shelf

Four words because one word could not carry it. The filesystem has one.
