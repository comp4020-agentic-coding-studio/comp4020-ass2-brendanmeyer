# Archive or Delete?

Keeping everything is not a retention policy.

It is the absence of one.

---

## The cost of keeping

Every version you keep is another candidate for being mistaken for the
current one.

Week 1's filename existed because four files were kept and none was marked.

---

## Decay doesn't wait

- Bit rot on failing media
- A format whose only reader stopped shipping
- A cited link that now returns a different page
- A cloud pointer that resolves to nothing
- Encryption whose key is no longer held

---

## Still in the directory. Still opens.

No longer contains what it did.

---

## Migration, not storage

Re-encode into currently readable formats.

Week 10's conversion damage, accepted deliberately — partial loss now
instead of total loss later.

---

## A non-lying system

- Currency is stated, not inferred
- History is recorded where the work happens
- Losses are logged
- Deletion is a decision with an author

---

## Why tooling didn't fix it

The four files called `final` were produced by people with access to version
control who didn't reach for it.

The workaround was cheaper at the moment of the save.

---

## The numbers

Google, across a disk population in the tens of thousands: drive failure is
poorly predicted by the signals people assume, and SMART alone will not tell
you which drive is about to go.

Harvard Law Review: more than 70% of URLs cited in law journals, and 50% in
US Supreme Court opinions, no longer resolve to what was cited.

The citation still looks fine. That is the problem.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Deciding is still human work. A well-built system refuses to pretend it
> has already happened.
